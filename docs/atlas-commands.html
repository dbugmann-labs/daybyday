The five
ways in

One command drives this pipeline. /atlas takes five forms, and three of them are entry points that differ in exactly one way: how much you have already decided. idea writes, backlog chooses, feature skips choosing. They converge on the same Feature issue at G1, and nothing downstream can tell which route it took.

How the five forms fit together

Three ways in, one rail out. /atlas idea is the only form that stops nothing and decides nothing; /atlas story is the only one that starts downstream of every product decision.

What each form does, and what comes out

Every form ends either at a stop you answer or at an artifact you can open. Nothing runs unattended past a gate.

FormWhat it doesWhat comes out
/atlas
/atlas next Reads pnpm run status and takes the next step from wherever the work actually is. On a story branch it reports that Story; anywhere else it reports the tracker and the backlog. Either a gate stop put to you, or an agent's work plus a fresh status. It creates nothing of its own.
/atlas idea <want> Captures one thing you want the app to do. Reads the backlog, the Decided ledger, the capability specs and the open Features first. Asks at most one question, and only about which want it is. One B-nnn entry in docs/backlog.md, committed on chore/backlog — or nothing at all, plus the reason: already an entry, already a shipped requirement, or already dropped.
/atlas backlog
/atlas backlog B-007 Grooms. Clusters the wants by the capability they would land in, proposes promote, merge, split or drop for each, forces a choice on anything two passes old, grills the cluster you pick. The B-007 form skips the clustering. A grooming stop, then G1. On approval: a Feature issue and its sub-issue edge, an Epic first if needed, the promoted entries moved to Decided, the backlog PR merged. Then G2, then G4. Every pass appends a dated line to the Grooming passes log, promotion or not.
/atlas feature <idea> The same intake for an idea that skips the backlog, because you already know it is one capability. Grills it, and says where it is thin. The same Feature issue in the same place. G1, then G2, then G4.
/atlas story <issue#> Gets onto that Story's worktree — cutting it if Stage 4 has not — and drives it from wherever it is: propose, red, green, review, archive, merge. Whatever the next gate is, G4 or G7, or a merged Story and a closed issue.
You do not have to remember to groom. pnpm run status, run anywhere but a story branch, prints the backlog under the issue tree — how many wants, how many decided, what has arrived since the last pass, and which entries have now survived two passes. Those last go straight into WAITING ON YOU beside the Features you owe a decomposition. It is counted off the Grooming passes log, which is why a pass that promoted nothing still gets written down: an unrecorded pass resets every want's age to zero.
Count the stops. idea is zero — no gate, no G-number, no tracker write. backlog is three before any code exists: the grooming stop, G1, G2. feature is two, because it has no grooming stop. From G4 onward all of them are the same four-interruption Story.

idea, backlog, feature — when each is right

Three points on one funnel. The only thing separating them is how much you have already decided.

/atlas idea

“I want this and I don't know what it is yet.”

You thought of something in the shower. You do not know whether it is a capability, half of one, or three. Use it liberally — the file is designed to be cheap to add to and expensive to leave alone. It writes your words verbatim and the outcome as the heading, never the mechanism, so nothing about the design has been decided by the act of capturing it.

COSTS ONE LINE · INTERRUPTS NOTHING
/atlas backlog

“I have twenty minutes and I want to decide what to build next.”

The raw material is everything idea has accumulated, and the clustering is the entire value. It is where three separate wants get spotted as one surface, where one entry turns out to be two, and where something that has sat through two passes forces you to admit you are never building it.

COSTS THREE STOPS · ENDS AT G4
/atlas feature

“I already know this is one capability and I want it now.”

A bug you decided to fix properly. Something the last Story made obviously necessary. It is deliberately a bypass, and what it bypasses is the clustering — so it is right exactly when there is nothing to cluster against.

COSTS TWO STOPS · ENDS AT G4
The failure mode worth naming is reaching for /atlas feature when /atlas backlog was the right call — it feels faster and it skips the one step that catches merges and splits. A Feature minted from a single idea in isolation is the horizontal slice you find out about three Stories later. The test: if I had not just thought of this, would it already be in the backlog? If yes, groom. If it genuinely arrived whole and separate, take the bypass.

The mirror failure is hoarding — forty entries and no grooming pass, which is the parking lot again with a better filename. The two-passes rule is the guard, but it only fires when /atlas backlog runs, so the cadence is yours. Roughly: groom whenever you are about to ask what next?
