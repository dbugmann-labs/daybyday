## Context

**Days away** are a span of dates a person says, over the commitments they pick, on which those
commitments owe nothing (`CONTEXT.md` § *Days away*). The Feature grill made a day away mean
everything a gap means: no row, a weekly quota owing only for the days it keeps, an every-N-days count
beginning again on the day back. The Story grill then made the span a thing of its own: listed, named
or not, removed whole before it starts, kept after it ends, carried by a copy, said on the day screen,
left by a commitment that is stopped or deleted, and kept through a change of rhythm.

A gap already exists in the roster, as two eras of one commitment with days between them, and every
reader of a commitment's days — a day's rows, a quota's standing and part weeks, a look-back — reads
it there. Until now every gap was in the past, because a stop and a resume are only ever made on the
day they are made.

## Decision

**The roster holds each days away beside its commitments, and every reader lays it over the eras.**
A days away carries an identity of its own, a name or none, a first and a last day, and the
identities of the commitments it is over, and nothing else. Saying, changing, removing or unticking
it edits the span alone and never an era: each commitment keeps its own life — its eras, stops and
take-ups as they were made, a change, stop or take-up made while away included. When the roster is
read, a day a span covers is **hidden** from each commitment it is over, unless the record holds
something of that commitment that day: a hidden day is due on no rhythm, holds no weekly-quota share
and reads as a gap day, and an every-N-days era is counted again from the day after the span. Every
reader — a day's rows, a quota's standing, a look-back, a shift — reads through that one rule.

The roster form moves to 10, adding the list of days away; an earlier form reads as holding none.
Form 11 keeps form 10's shape over eras no span has cut; form 10 is read as written, its cut gaps
standing as stops and take-ups. A copy nests the roster form as written and keeps its own form.

## Consequences

- **Removing gives back exactly what was hidden**, by construction: nothing was taken from the eras,
  so an every-N-days count runs on unbroken, and a change made since stands from the day it was made.
- **No move, in any order, can give back or take away anything but the span's own days**, and the
  every-N-days count begun again after them. A stop or a take-up inside a span stays on its day
  whatever the span later does, a stopped commitment stays picked wherever the span moves, and a span
  moved back hides exactly what it hid.
- **A span can be refused for what follows it.** Saying, moving or removing one moves the day an
  every-N-days count begins again; where a recorded day would fall off it, the act is refused, naming
  the commitment and the day, as a change of rhythm is — removal included.
- **A record is never hidden.** A day holding one shows its row under days away, and is hidden again
  once the record is taken back; nothing is stored for it.
- **Every reader of a commitment's days must read days away.** One that reads the eras alone owes a
  hidden day; the readers are named in `change-days-away`'s `design.md`, and each is tested at its own
  seam.

## Alternatives considered

- **Gaps cut into the eras, the span beside them.** This record's first choice. It needs no new
  reader, but a removal or a move must re-form eras the cut destroyed, which cannot tell a span's gap
  from a stop and a take-up; remembering each stop on the span was tried and failed review eight times.
- **Gaps alone, with no span.** Rejected at the Story grill: a span's gap looks exactly like a pause,
  and nothing could list it, remove it whole or say it on the day screen.
- **The span in a store of its own.** It would not be written with the eras it hides, and a copy
  would need a sixth place.
